# ADR-0029: Revocation refunds the card, and the ledger records what was refunded

Story 38 and ADR-0004 promise a struck-off Practitioner "a prorated refund for the unused portion of my billing period". The handler cancelled with `prorate: true, invoice_now: true` and sent an email saying they had been refunded, but `stripe.refunds.create` appeared nowhere in the codebase (issue #69). **Proration is not a refund.** It writes a credit onto the customer's Stripe balance, to be drawn down by a future invoice — and a revoked Practitioner has no future invoice, by construction. The money stayed with MiCare and the email said otherwise.

**The card is the refund channel, so the cancel is deliberately un-prorated.** `subscriptions.cancel` is now called with `prorate: false`, and the unused portion is put back on the card with `refunds.create`. Doing both would compensate twice: a proration credit *and* a card refund for the same unused days. One channel, and it is the one the Practitioner can actually spend.

**MiCare computes the amount rather than reading Stripe's credit.** The refund is `unusedPortionPence` (`src/refund-amount.ts`) over the latest **paid** invoice's `amount_paid` and the period its line item bought — the line's period, not the invoice's `period_start`/`period_end`, which describe when items accrued and coincide with the service window only for a plain one-line renewal. The share is rounded **down**, so a fraction of a penny stays with MiCare rather than being invented for the refund. A zero result short-circuits before Stripe: `refunds.create` without an `amount` refunds the *whole* charge, so "nothing left to refund" must never reach the API as a call.

**Three outcomes, and the email states which one happened.** `refunded` quotes the actual figure (`£14.50`), `nothing-to-refund` cancelled a subscription with no refundable remainder (no paid invoice, or the period already elapsed), and `not-billed` had no live subscription at all. The old copy promised "the unused portion of your current billing period" unconditionally; per ADR-0019 the email may claim only what MiCare has done, and now it names the number.

**The ledger row is written `pending` and settled afterwards.** ADR-0008 records before acting, to guarantee no double-cancellation, and accepts that a failure after the write is never retried. That trade-off is materially heavier now that a card refund sits inside the window. The first write no longer asserts an outcome it cannot know yet: it claims the `practitioner_id` (so a replay is still a no-op — the property ADR-0008 exists for) with `outcome = 'pending'`, and the real outcome plus `refunded_pence` are written once the Stripe work settles. This gives up the "append-only" property stated in ADR-0008 and migration 0005, and buys something better than the structured log line it recommended for recovery: `select * from public.revocation_refunds where outcome = 'pending'` **is** the list of revocations whose money needs a human. Retrying automatically is still refused — a retry that cannot tell "the refund never happened" from "the refund happened and the process died" is how a customer gets paid twice.

**Under `VITE_STRIPE_MOCK` the unused portion comes from the local billing cycle** and the single £29 price, exactly as ADR-0011 derives the billing period locally when there is no subscription to read. The alternative — reporting `nothing-to-refund` under the mock — would leave the refunded branch unexercised in dev and E2E, and would put a false sentence in the dev-mode email. No real money is in reach of that path.
